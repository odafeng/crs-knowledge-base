Zanzalintinib plus Atezolizumab versus Regorafenib in Refractory Metastatic Colorectal Cancer (STELLAR-303)

Journal: Lancet 2025
DOI: 10.1016/S0140-6736(25)02025-2
PMID: 41130252
Study: STELLAR-303 (Phase 3 RCT)
Note: Full text is available at https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(25)02025-2.

Abstract

Design: Global Phase 3 randomized controlled trial in patients with refractory (third-line or later) microsatellite-stable / mismatch-repair-proficient (MSS/pMMR) metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) who had progressed on or were intolerant to standard therapies.

Arms: Patients (N = 901) were randomized to:

  • Experimental: zanzalintinib (a multitargeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor of MET/VEGFR/AXL/MER/RET) + atezolizumab (anti-PD-L1)
  • Control: regorafenib

Primary endpoint: Overall survival (OS) in the intention-to-treat (ITT) population and OS in the subset of patients without liver metastases.

Key Results

Endpoint Zanzalintinib + Atezolizumab Regorafenib
Median OS (ITT) 10.9 months 9.4 months
OS HR (ITT) 0.80 (95% CI 0.69–0.93; P=0.0045)
OS HR (no liver metastases, interim) 0.79 (P=0.087, not significant; final analysis pending)
Grade ≥3 treatment-related AEs 60% 37%

Conclusions

STELLAR-303 is the first Phase 3 trial to demonstrate a statistically significant overall survival benefit for an immunotherapy-based combination versus regorafenib in MSS/pMMR metastatic colorectal cancer, challenging the long-held view that MSS mCRC does not respond to immune checkpoint inhibition. Zanzalintinib is hypothesized to remodel the tumor immune microenvironment, acting synergistically with PD-L1 blockade. The OS improvement (HR 0.80) is modest relative to current third-line standards such as SUNLIGHT (TAS-102 + bevacizumab, HR 0.61) and FRESCO-2 (fruquintinib, HR 0.66), and toxicity was notably higher (Grade ≥3 treatment-related AEs 60% vs 37%). The no-liver-metastases subset did not reach significance at interim analysis. Despite these caveats, the study is an important milestone that may drive a new wave of research into later-line immunotherapy for MSS mCRC.
